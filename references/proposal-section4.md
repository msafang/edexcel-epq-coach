# Proposal Section 4: Resources for Research, Write-up, and Presentation

Use this reference when drafting or reviewing the Edexcel Proposal field about resources. The answer should explain what each resource is for and how it will be judged or used. It should not become a long inventory of source types.

## Three resource functions

When the form asks what resources are needed for research, write-up, and presentation, cover all three functions:

| Function | Typical resources | What the plan should explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | journals, books, official statistics, reports, company material, data, equipment, participants | what question or task the resource supports and how its quality will be checked |
| Write-up | word-processing software, notes, reference manager or organised reference record | how the student will draft, organise evidence, track sources, cite, and produce a bibliography |
| Presentation | presentation software, slides, speaker notes, display or demonstration equipment | how the student will communicate the process, outcome, decisions, and evaluation |

Use only tools and access routes the student actually has or can reasonably confirm. Do not invent a school subscription, database, participant, device, software licence, or specialist facility.

## Match resources to the research question

For each important resource class, state:

`resource -> purpose -> access route -> evaluation/use -> limitation or contingency`

Source variety is not a quality target by itself. Choose resources because they answer the question, support the method, or enable the outcome. Do not add social media, forums, generic online articles, or conference papers merely to make the list look broad. Include them only when they are genuinely relevant to the question and the student can explain their evidential status.

For a P301 literature-based project, academic journal articles may be the main evidence, books or textbooks may establish foundational concepts, and selected secondary cases may show how theories relate to practice. For a P302 project, include the data, instrument, equipment, software, sample/participants, permissions, storage, and analysis resources needed for the investigation. For P303/P304, include the relevant materials, venue, equipment, collaborators, rehearsal/production space, testing, and safety resources.

## Source hierarchy and distinct functions

Do not treat all sources as equally reliable or give every source class the same role:

- **Peer-reviewed academic journal articles:** core theories, arguments, methods, and research evidence. Conference papers can be included when relevant and accessible, but are not automatically necessary for a school-level literature review.
- **Books and textbooks:** definitions, background knowledge, and introductory explanations before reading more specialised research. They do not automatically settle a contested claim.
- **Official statistics and regulator/government reports:** measured data, policy, market conditions, or official definitions. Name the issuing body when known.
- **Industry reports:** market trends, prices, competition, and market-share information from industry associations, research firms, or other sector sources. Do not label these as official unless they are published by an official body.
- **Company reports and announcements:** what a company reports about its own strategy, performance, or decisions. They can provide case evidence but are not independent evidence of the company's motives; consider purpose and possible bias and cross-check important claims where possible.
- **Business news:** dated reporting and context for events or competitive responses. Check the original evidence, author, date, and whether the report distinguishes fact from interpretation.
- **Search and research tools:** Google Scholar, school/library databases, catalogues, reference lists, note systems, or reference managers. These help locate and organise evidence; a search tool is not itself evidence.

Use the strongest source available for each claim. If a source supplies only background or an example, do not use it as proof of a psychological motive, causal relationship, or general conclusion.

## Search and reference management

The plan should say how the student will find and manage sources, not only which sources they hope to cite:

- use initial keywords linked to the question and develop additional keywords as the research progresses;
- use useful papers' reference lists and citation trails carefully, checking the original source;
- keep a record of useful and rejected references, relevance, credibility, purpose, limitations, and where each source may be used;
- maintain citations and bibliography from the beginning rather than generating them at the end;
- distinguish a search lead, a source read, a source selected for evidence, and a claim supported by that source.

Do not claim that a source is peer-reviewed, reliable, current, or accessible without checking it. Do not use AI-generated references, summaries, data, or source evaluations as student evidence.

## Write-up and presentation resources are part of the answer

For the write-up, name the practical tools the student will use to draft, revise, store notes, organise references, and check citation-bibliography consistency. Do not turn the answer into a software recommendation list; the purpose is the workflow.

For the presentation, name the tool or equipment needed to organise findings and explain the research process. The plan should not promise presentation evidence, slides, or a demonstration that the student has not yet created. For a P303/P304 outcome, include the presentation or demonstration resources relevant to the performance or artefact.

## Review checks

Before presenting Section 4 as final, check:

1. Research, write-up, and presentation resources are all addressed when the form asks for them.
2. Each important resource has a clear purpose linked to an objective, method, claim, or outcome.
3. Academic, official, industry, company, news, and search-tool roles are distinguished accurately.
4. Sources are evaluated for relevance, authority, currency, accuracy, purpose, bias, limitations, and suitability; the plan does not equate variety with quality.
5. Resources and access routes are realistic and not invented.
6. Social media, forums, and generic online articles are included only when they directly answer the question and their evidential limits are explicit.
7. Reference management begins during research and can generate Activity Log evidence.
8. The plan contains a practical alternative if a critical source, database, dataset, tool, or equipment route fails.
9. P301 is not forced into primary-data resources, while P302 has a describable data and method route.

Use `Unanswered`, `Initial`, `Executable`, or `Strong` for teaching feedback. Do not assign a fixed numeric score from the resource list alone.

## Output modes

For a student, return a concise resource plan grouped by research, write-up, and presentation, followed by the evaluation approach and any missing access facts. For a teacher, report question-resource mismatches, unsupported source claims, missing tools, availability risks, and whether the plan supports the stated project type. For direct generation, use `[TO CONFIRM]` for databases, software, equipment, permissions, and sources not supplied by the student.
