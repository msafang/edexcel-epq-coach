# Proposal Research Areas

Use this reference when the Proposal asks `What areas of research will you cover?` or a similar question. The field should describe the academic or professional areas the student expects to explore, not simply list case studies, sources, or every subject that might appear in the final paper.

## Research areas versus evidence and cases

Keep three levels separate:

| Level | Meaning | Example for a price-war project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Academic area | a field of ideas or knowledge | Psychology, Economics, Business Management |
| Related possible area | a plausible field that may become relevant | Behavioural Economics, Marketing/Consumer Behaviour |
| Evidence or application | where theories will be observed or tested | food-delivery and new-energy-vehicle price competition |

Cases and industries should not be counted as academic areas merely to reach four items. Three well-defined areas can be stronger than five loosely related labels. Conversely, at Proposal stage the student may identify additional possible areas if they are clearly marked as provisional and not presented as confirmed parts of the final paper.

## Open Proposal-stage scope

The research has not formally started when the Proposal is written. Separate current expectations from later discoveries:

- `main areas I expect to cover` for fields already justified by the question and initial understanding;
- `related areas I may need to explore` for plausible intersections that require further reading;
- `selected examples/cases I may use as evidence` for applications, not research areas.

Use plan-stage wording such as `I expect`, `I may`, `I will explore whether`, and `as my research develops`. Add a boundary explaining that the list may be narrowed or refined after initial research. This should not become permission for unlimited scope: retain a central question, a manageable project window, and a decision rule for dropping areas that do not help answer the question.

## Give each area a distinct job

Do not list several fields followed by the same generic sentence. Explain how each area contributes:

- **Psychology / behavioural decision-making:** how individual or managerial decision-makers may respond to gains, losses, uncertainty, pressure, competition, or escalation.
- **Economics:** rational profit maximisation, game theory, oligopoly, strategic interaction, and when price changes may be economically rational.
- **Business Management:** pricing and competitive strategy, market share, firm responses, and managerial decision-making in organisations.
- **Behavioural Economics:** a possible intersection for comparing traditional rational models with behavioural explanations; include it as a separate area only when the student can explain why it is needed.
- **Marketing / Consumer Behaviour:** a possible supporting area for demand, customer choices, brand position, or market response. Do not make it central when the question is mainly about firms' decisions unless the student confirms that consumer behaviour is part of the question.

These are examples of role separation, not a fixed list. For another topic, assign each area a distinct contribution to the question or brief.

## Avoid pre-deciding the conclusion

Do not assign one area only to initiation and another only to continuation if the research question compares explanations of both. For example, rational economic theories may help explain how price wars begin and whether continuation can also be strategic; behavioural ideas may offer another explanation for continued competition when profits are falling. The Proposal should leave the relative explanatory power open for the literature review and discussion to assess.

Do not describe `losing money` unless the student has evidence that the firms are making an accounting loss. `falling profits`, `lower short-term profits`, or `price reductions that may reduce profits` may be more accurate, depending on the intended claim.

## Search development and Activity Log handoff

Initial areas and keywords should be treated as provisional. The plan should allow the student to:

`initial areas -> initial search -> new concept or field -> scope decision -> Activity Log record`

If a new area emerges, record why it was added, which objective it serves, what it displaces or narrows, and how the timeline/resources change. If an area proves irrelevant, recording its reduction or removal is valid project development. Do not silently rewrite the original Proposal to match the final paper.

## Review checks

Before presenting the research-areas answer as final, check:

1. The answer identifies genuine academic/professional areas rather than counting cases or source types as areas.
2. Each area has a distinct role linked to the central question, objectives, method, or outcome.
3. Main areas and possible related areas are clearly distinguished.
4. The number of areas is manageable and not inflated by weakly related disciplines.
5. The wording reflects that research has not formally started and leaves room for justified development.
6. The answer does not pre-decide which theory will explain initiation or continuation best.
7. Cases and industries are presented as secondary evidence or application, not as equal-level academic areas.
8. Any future scope change can be recorded truthfully in the Activity Log.

Use `Unanswered`, `Initial`, `Executable`, or `Strong` for teaching feedback. Do not assign a numeric score because the answer names three, four, or five areas.

## Output modes

For a student, return main areas, possible related areas, the role of each, and a short scope boundary. For a teacher, report level-mixing, duplicated roles, unsupported disciplines, premature conclusions, and scope risks. For direct generation, preserve `[TO CONFIRM]` for areas that depend on initial research or student access and do not present cases as academic fields.
