# Activity Log Writing and Process Evidence

Use this reference when drafting, reviewing, or lightly editing an Edexcel EPQ Activity Log. It focuses on truthful process evidence, intellectual development, and natural student-facing language. It is not an instruction to invent entries, increase the number of rows, or make every entry sound different for its own sake.

## What a strong log makes visible

An Activity Log should allow a reader to follow:

`planned intention -> action -> evidence/output -> problem or uncertainty -> decision/response -> effect on the plan`

Not every entry needs every element, but the log as a whole should show more than a list of completed tasks. Valuable process evidence includes:

- narrowing or revising the topic and research question;
- changes to search terms, research areas, source selection, or source rejection;
- a tutor or supervisor comment and the student's response to it;
- a delay, failed search, unavailable resource, method issue, or data-quality problem;
- a reasoned decision to change scope, structure, method, evidence, or schedule;
- a comparison of competing explanations or an emerging counterargument;
- drafting, re-drafting, checking, referencing, presentation, or production decisions that affect the outcome.

Use concrete supporting evidence where it exists: a timetable or Gantt chart, search record, source comparison table, research notes, rejected-source record, draft version, feedback, calculation, data file, design iteration, or presentation material. Do not name evidence that the student has not supplied or that cannot be located.

## A flexible entry structure

For a real entry, establish as applicable:

1. **Date and action:** what the student actually did, in chronological order.
2. **Evidence/output:** what was produced, changed, read, tested, rejected, or recorded.
3. **Purpose:** how the activity served the question, objective, method, or outcome.
4. **Issue or decision:** what became uncertain, difficult, newly important, or worth changing.
5. **Response and effect:** what the student did about it and how the plan, source choice, method, scope, or interpretation changed.
6. **Next action:** only when a real next action is due or when the form has a required next-step field.

The entry may end after a result or decision. Do not force a next step if it adds no information. Conversely, do not remove an explicit next action when it demonstrates a meaningful transition, dependency, or follow-up.

## Natural endings

Alternate endings according to what the evidence supports:

- **Next-action ending:** use when the next task genuinely follows from the current decision.
- **Decision ending:** use when the important evidence is that the student chose, rejected, narrowed, or reorganised something.
- **Reflection ending:** use when the student learned a specific process lesson from the activity.
- **Open-question ending:** use when the activity creates a question that directs the next research decision.
- **Result ending:** use when the completed action has a clear outcome and no separate next step is needed.

This is not a request to use five fixed templates in rotation. Keep the wording natural, use the student's own level of English, and avoid identical sentence rhythms across a long sequence. A final proofread may remove repetition, tense errors, irrelevant sentences, or awkward connections, but should not flatten every entry into the same polished voice.

## Student voice and timing

Write what the student could truthfully have known at that time. Do not place a concept, source, conclusion, or polished interpretation into an earlier entry merely because it appears in the finished paper. If a later discovery changed the plan, record that change at the point it happened or mark the historical detail `[TO CONFIRM]`.

Preserve a capable student's natural variation without deliberately adding mistakes. A log can be clear and reflective without sounding like a university dissertation or a perfect retrospective narrative. Do not over-edit a near-final log when the remaining issues are only stylistic; prioritise factual accuracy, relevance, chronology, and the connection to visible evidence.

## Process evidence versus later evaluation

Keep the functions distinct:

- **Activity Log:** what happened, what the student decided, how the plan changed, and what evidence was produced at the time.
- **Evaluation/Reflection:** what the completed project achieved, how the plan compared with reality overall, what limitations affected the outcome, and what should change next time.

An Activity Log entry may contain a specific reflection such as “this showed me that...” when it follows from the recorded activity. It should not become a general essay about time management, critical thinking, or improved English without a concrete before/after event. Save broad synthesis for the Evaluation/Reflection.

## Tutor feedback and independence

Record tutor or supervisor feedback accurately and show the student's response. Feedback may prompt a question, revision, or scope decision, but do not write as though the tutor selected the student's conclusion or completed the analysis. The log should make the student's choices, work, and response visible.

## Review checklist

Before accepting or generating a log, check:

- dates and sequence are real or clearly marked for confirmation;
- entries contain observable activity and supporting evidence, not only claims of progress;
- the question and objectives remain visible across the project;
- problems lead to a response, decision, or justified plan change where relevant;
- source, method, scope, and schedule changes are explainable;
- later discoveries are not falsely presented as early intentions;
- closing sentences are varied because the underlying function varies, not because a template was mechanically rotated;
- no entry is padded with an unrelated next-step sentence;
- the overall log shows planning, monitoring, decision-making, adaptation, and progress toward the outcome;
- missing facts, dates, evidence, tutor comments, or personal learning are marked `[待确认]`/`[TO CONFIRM]` rather than invented.

For a teacher-facing review, report the strongest process evidence, the largest evidence gaps, and three high-value repairs. Do not assess authenticity from prose style alone.
