# Proposal Coaching Protocol

Use this reference for a guided Edexcel Proposal session. It converts the supplied `PROPOSAL_SKILL_DESIGN.md` into a standalone skill workflow. The checks below are teaching heuristics that support the Edexcel assessment objectives; they are not additional official mark requirements.

## Stage 0 Readiness Gate

Before asking the student to complete the form, establish:

- the project type: P301, P302, P303, or P304;
- one confirmed working question, hypothesis, commission, or design brief rather than several unresolved topics;
- the scope: object/case, period, place, population, variables, or central creative problem as applicable;
- a realistic outcome for the available time and scale;
- an initial source, data, material, venue, equipment, or participant route;
- any school approval, privacy, safeguarding, consent, or safety issue.

If a material gate fails, return to topic/method selection and state what must be decided before continuing. Do not fill the remaining fields with a polished but unworkable plan.

## Four-part feedback for every field

For each question or logical block, return these four items:

1. **Purpose:** what the field is intended to demonstrate in the project and, where useful, which AO it supports.
2. **Strong-answer ingredients:** the facts, boundaries, decisions, and evidence the response should contain.
3. **Current gap or risk:** what is vague, unsupported, inconsistent, unrealistic, or not yet confirmed.
4. **Next revision:** one to three concrete questions or actions for the student.

Use an anonymized structural example only when it clarifies the form. Never encourage copying an example's subject, wording, sources, or personal experience.

## Staged interaction

Run one logical block at a time rather than requesting a finished Proposal in one turn. A practical sequence is:

1. Basic information and project type.
2. Title/question/hypothesis/brief and objectives.
3. Personal, subject, and future-plan reasons.
4. Responsibilities, independence, and use of tutor feedback.
5. Activities, dependencies, durations, milestones, and buffer time.
6. Resources, source/data/material evaluation, and research areas.
7. Expected outcome and completion/success criteria.
8. Risks, ethics/safety, contingencies, and alternatives.

The skill can draft after each block, but must label unconfirmed facts and wait for confirmation before presenting a field as final. If the surrounding product has version history, preserve the original answer, revision, and confirmed version; if it does not, return a concise change summary instead of claiming that the system saved history.

## Module standards

### A. Basic information

Keep learner, project type, title, dates, and tutor fields consistent with the project record. Do not put unconfirmed claims into identity or approval fields. Check that any English and Chinese titles have the same meaning.

### B. Title, question, and objectives

The title should be a focused question, hypothesis, commission, or design brief that can be answered or realised within the available scope. For Proposal Section 1 / Project Objectives, also read [Proposal Section 1](proposal-section1.md): write a clear Level 3 student plan, retain necessary subject terminology, reduce unnecessary dissertation-style meta-language, keep the eventual judgement open, and explicitly answer each prompt shown by the form. Objectives should identify the object, central issue, boundary, intended comparison/evaluation/interpretation, method, and outcome. Judge completeness before length; the word ranges in the Section 1 reference are internal drafting heuristics only. They may be broken into two to four answerable subquestions when that improves control of the work, but this is a planning recommendation, not an Edexcel quota.

Reject objectives that merely repeat the topic, promise several unrelated industries/periods/populations, or imply causal certainty from descriptive evidence. Keep the topic, research question/hypothesis, method, and outcome as separate fields.

### C. Reasons for choosing the project

Read [Proposal Section 2](proposal-section2.md). Build a truthful connection between a specific interest or prior observation, an academic or real-world question, and a subject/future-study goal. Explain why this particular question, case, performance, or artefact was chosen, rather than repeating Section 1's objectives or skills. Check theoretical accuracy: strategic behaviour is not automatically irrational, and short-term lower profits may be consistent with a longer-term objective. Calibrate `future study` wording to the student's confirmed plans and do not turn a possible interest into a definite future direction. Do not turn a general claim of social importance into an unsupported impact claim.

### D. Responsibilities and independence

Describe what the student will decide and produce: topic refinement, source/data/material choice, analysis, writing or making, revision, and presentation. Describe the tutor's role as guidance, questioning, and feedback. Do not generate a blanket claim that the student will work independently if the actual support arrangement is unknown. Tutor and proposal-checker approvals remain human fields.

### E. Activities and timescales

Read [Proposal Section 3](proposal-section3.md). Every major activity should have a task, purpose/output, duration or date range, dependency, review point, risk, and adjustment route. Use a manageable set of high-level stages and separate ongoing Activity Log, reference management, tutor review, and approval/safety activities. Durations may overlap and should not be added as if every task were strictly serial. The plan must cover research/design, making or data collection where relevant, analysis, drafting or production, revision, evaluation, and presentation. Include two or three checkable milestones as a planning aid, leave buffer time, and keep the final writing/rehearsal from being squeezed into the last day.

Unit routing:

- **P301:** question refinement, source search/evaluation, reading and synthesis, outline, drafting, revision, bibliography, evaluation, and presentation.
- **P302:** all relevant P301-like planning plus method design, sample/participant or dataset access, data collection, cleaning/analysis, calculations or modelling, and contingency work.
- **P303:** idea exploration, research, development/rehearsal, presentation of the performance, evaluation, and any venue/safety/participant requirements.
- **P304:** research, idea development, material/equipment access, production of the artefact, testing or user feedback where relevant, evaluation, and safety/contingency requirements.

### F. Resources and research method

Read [Proposal Section 4](proposal-section4.md). Name research, write-up, and presentation resources and their purpose. For each important research resource, state how relevance, authority, currency, accuracy, bias, purpose, and limitations will be checked, what question it helps answer, and how it can be accessed. Distinguish peer-reviewed, textbook, official, industry, company, news, and search-tool roles. “Google”, “online material”, “books”, or “teacher help” alone is not a usable resource plan. Do not add social media or source types merely for variety, and do not paste URLs without explaining their role.

For P301, do not force a first-hand data section. For P302, make data access, sample/instrument/procedure, consent/privacy, storage, and analysis visible. For P303/P304, make materials, equipment, collaborators, rehearsal/production space, permissions, and safety visible where applicable.

### G. Expected outcome and success criteria

State the actual deliverable and how it will answer the question or fulfil the brief. A P301 outcome is a dissertation or critical literature-based study; a P302 outcome is an investigation/field study with analyzable evidence; P303 and P304 require the relevant performance or artefact plus its supporting explanation. Success criteria should be observable, such as answering each objective, presenting a defensible interpretation, completing a reproducible analysis, or meeting defined production constraints. Avoid promises of a predetermined result.

### H. Risks, ethics, and alternatives

For each material risk, state prevention, trigger, response, and effect on scope or interpretation. Typical risks include insufficient or conflicting sources, an over-large question, unavailable participants/data/equipment, time compression, privacy/consent issues, unsafe activity, and a project that becomes descriptive without analysis. Sensible alternatives include narrowing the scope, changing sources, changing the sample, replacing a survey with a literature-based approach where appropriate, or revising the question. Record ethical or safety escalation rather than inventing approval.

## Cross-field consistency gate

After all blocks, check the following relationships and show contradictions before rewriting anything:

| Relationship | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Title/question ↔ objectives | Every objective contributes to answering or realising the stated title/brief. |
| Objectives ↔ activities | Each major activity produces evidence for at least one objective. |
| Activities ↔ time | Order, dependencies, durations, milestones, and buffer time are realistic. |
| Objectives ↔ resources | The planned sources/data/materials can support the objectives and are accessible. |
| Method ↔ outcome | The method can actually produce the stated outcome and level of certainty. |
| Risks ↔ alternatives | Each major risk has a practical response and scope consequence. |
| Proposal ↔ later records | The Activity Log can record execution, problems, decisions, and changes against this plan. |
| Section 1 ↔ Section 2 | Section 1 answers what the project will answer/find out/learn; Section 2 explains why this student chose it, without avoidable repetition. |
| Section 3 ↔ later records | The activity plan creates realistic dated evidence for the Activity Log and leaves room for decisions, feedback, problems, and changes. |
| Section 3 ↔ Section 4 | Planned activities have the resources, access routes, tools, and contingencies needed to carry them out. |
| Section 4 ↔ outcome | The selected resources can support the stated outcome and level of certainty; write-up and presentation tools are included where the form asks for them. |
| Proposal ↔ paper/presentation | The question, project type, terminology, and conclusion/presentation plan remain aligned after revisions. |

If there is a contradiction, name both fields and ask the student to choose which one to change. Do not silently harmonise the Proposal and erase the decision history.

## Four-level field quality scale

Use this compact scale for student and teacher views:

- **未回答 / Unanswered:** no usable response or only a label.
- **初步回答 / Initial:** direction exists, but facts, boundaries, feasibility, or evidence are missing.
- **可执行回答 / Executable:** specific and feasible, with basic alignment to the other fields.
- **强回答 / Strong:** specific, bounded, feasible, evidence-aware, and proactive about risk and alternatives.

Always give `已经具备`, `缺少或风险`, and `下一步`. Do not translate this scale into an official numeric mark.

## Proposal completion gate

Only recommend moving to Activity Log work when the following are confirmed:

- the title and question/hypothesis/brief are settled;
- objectives are more than a restatement of the topic;
- scope, method, and outcome agree;
- activities, dependencies, dates, and milestones are executable;
- resources are concrete and have a basic evaluation method;
- key risks have alternatives or an escalation route;
- the student confirms the plan is truthful and theirs;
- required human approval/signature fields are identified for later completion.

## Output modes

### Student view

Return the confirmed Proposal draft, the purpose and quality level for each block, facts to verify, a summary of scope/objectives/activities/resources/risks, and the first Activity Log tasks.

### Teacher view

Return completeness and feasibility findings, project-type/method judgement, quality levels, cross-field contradictions, evidence gaps, before/after changes when supplied, and items requiring teacher approval, signature, safeguarding, or re-selection.
