# Proposal Section 3: Activities and Timescales

Use this reference when drafting or reviewing the activity plan in an Edexcel Proposal. The plan should show how the student can complete the project and how the plan can develop. It should not look like a perfect timetable reconstructed from the finished paper.

## What the section should demonstrate

Section 3 should make visible:

- the main work needed to answer the question or fulfil the brief;
- the intended output or decision from each activity;
- realistic order, duration, dependencies, review points, and buffer time;
- how the student will respond when sources, data, materials, time, or the first plan do not work;
- how the plan will create evidence for the Activity Log and later evaluation.

Use the actual school calendar, centre deadlines, and project stage when available. There is no universal official total number of weeks for this reference. Do not treat an example's 19, 22, or 25 weeks as a requirement.

## Use a two-layer plan

For a student-facing Proposal, use a small number of high-level stages plus a few clear ongoing activities:

```text
major stages: decide -> plan -> research/method -> develop -> analyse/write/make -> review -> present
ongoing streams: Activity Log + reference/research notes + key tutor reviews + approvals/safety checks
```

The high-level plan can use approximately nine stages when that helps the student understand the project:

1. Decide the topic and refine the research question, hypothesis, commission, or design brief.
2. Plan the project and complete the Proposal.
3. Find, access, and evaluate sources, data, materials, equipment, or participants.
4. Develop the introduction/background or initial design/performance approach.
5. Develop the main review, investigation, making, or performance work.
6. Analyse evidence, compare explanations, or test and improve the developing outcome.
7. Draft the conclusion and complete the separate project evaluation/reflection.
8. Review a full draft or complete outcome, use feedback, and make justified improvements.
9. Prepare and rehearse the presentation or final demonstration.

Adapt or combine stages for P302, P303, and P304. Do not force a literature-review structure onto an investigation, performance, or artefact.

## What each row needs

Each major row should state, as far as the student knows:

`activity -> purpose/output -> duration or date range -> dependency/review point -> contingency`

Use realistic descriptions such as `draft the main sections comparing the explanations` rather than mechanically listing every final chapter. If the final outcome is itself a critical literature review, avoid describing a separate “write the literature review” task unless it is genuinely a named section of the final document; use `develop the main review sections` or equivalent when that is more accurate.

Durations do not need to be strictly additive. Source searching and reading may overlap; bibliography and research notes should begin early and continue; drafting may overlap with further reading; tutor feedback may occur at several checkpoints. Show overlap or dependencies rather than making the total look precise by adding every row as if it were serial.

## Ongoing evidence streams

State these once below the table or attach them to relevant rows instead of pretending they happen only at the end:

- **Activity Log:** update it throughout the project with real dates, completed work, evidence/output, problems, decisions, plan changes, tutor feedback, and next actions.
- **Reference and research record:** keep useful references, search terms, notes, source evaluations, rejected sources, and new questions as the research develops.
- **Tutor or supervisor review:** review progress at meaningful checkpoints such as the Proposal, early research, method or structure, full draft/developing outcome, and final preparation. Record only feedback that actually occurred.
- **Approval, ethics, privacy, safeguarding, and safety checks:** place them before the activity that depends on approval or access.

Do not make `submit the Activity Log` the first or only Activity Log activity. Do not backfill a fictitious diary at the end.

## Allow research development

At Proposal stage, initial keywords, theories, cases, and sources are provisional. A strong plan can say that the student will develop additional search terms as new concepts emerge. Later Activity Log entries can show:

`initial search -> new concept/source -> keyword or framework change -> decision about scope -> effect on the plan`

This is more truthful than listing every theory that appears in the final paper at the start. Do not use this principle to excuse an unbounded project: the student still needs an initial scope, a credible resource/method route, and a way to decide when to stop expanding.

## Unit-specific planning

- **P301:** include question refinement, source search/evaluation, reading and synthesis, an outline or working structure, drafting, revision, referencing, evaluation, and presentation. Secondary case evidence is acceptable where it serves the literature-based question; primary data is not required by this planning guide.
- **P302:** add method design, access/consent, sample or dataset, data collection, data cleaning/analysis, calculations or modelling, uncertainty, and a response if recruitment, equipment, or data quality fails.
- **P303:** include research, concept development, rehearsal, feedback, performance/venue/participant requirements, safety, presentation, and evaluation.
- **P304:** include research, design development, materials/equipment access, production, testing or user feedback where appropriate, revision, safety, presentation, and evaluation.

Keep `conclusion` and `project evaluation` conceptually distinct: the conclusion answers the question or fulfils the brief; the evaluation reflects on management, method, resources, decisions, limitations, and learning.

## Review checks

Before presenting Section 3 as final, check:

1. The activities answer the question or fulfil the brief and are linked to objectives.
2. The order and dependencies are understandable, but overlapping work is not falsely presented as strictly serial.
3. Durations and dates match the real project window and leave time for failed searches, feedback, revision, and presentation.
4. The plan includes ongoing Activity Log and reference management, not only final submission.
5. Initial keywords, theories, and sources are appropriately provisional; later development can be recorded.
6. Tutor feedback and approvals occur at plausible checkpoints and are not invented.
7. Writing or production tasks describe real work rather than blindly reproducing the final paper's headings.
8. Each material risk has a response, scope consequence, or escalation route.
9. The plan provides evidence that can later support Activity Log, Evaluation, and AO1 management judgements.

Use `Unanswered`, `Initial`, `Executable`, or `Strong` for teaching feedback. Do not assign a fixed numeric score from the schedule alone.

## Output modes

For a student, return a concise table with activity, purpose/output, duration/date, and any key review or contingency, followed by the ongoing activities. For a teacher, report missing dependencies, implausible serial timing, absent ongoing evidence, weak contingencies, and conflicts with Section 1, Section 2, the project type, or the centre calendar. For direct generation, use `[TO CONFIRM]` for dates, tutor meetings, approvals, and access facts that were not supplied.
