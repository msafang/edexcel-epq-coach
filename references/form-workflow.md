# Proposal Activity Log and Evaluation Workflow

Use this reference when drafting or reviewing the three student records. The wording can be generated only from supplied facts. The examples below are structural patterns, not facts to copy.

## Input contract

Before writing, collect as much of the following as is available:

```yaml
unit: P301 or P302
language: English or requested language
title_or_topic: "..."
research_question_or_hypothesis: "..."
objectives: ["...", "..."]
personal_and_subject_reasons: ["...", "..."]
method: "literature review / survey / interview / experiment / field study / ..."
outcome: "..."
resources_and_evaluation: ["source type, purpose, credibility check"]
timeline: [{task: "...", planned_dates: "...", duration: "..."}]
real_log_facts: [{date: "...", action: "...", evidence: "...", issue: "...", response: "...", next_step: "..."}]
evaluation_facts: ["aim achieved...", "limitation...", "change next time..."]
presentation_evidence: "..."
```

If a field is absent, use `[TO CONFIRM]` in the draft and report the missing input. Never turn a plausible plan into a claimed completed action.

## Proposal field guide

### Title and question

The title should be a question, commission, or design brief that can be answered within the available time and word limit. Test it with:

1. Can the student name the population/case, period, or variables that define the scope?
2. Does the wording imply a method the student can actually complete?
3. Can the final conclusion answer the same wording without changing the subject?

Avoid titles that are only a broad theme, contain several unrelated questions, or promise causal certainty from descriptive evidence.

### Objectives

Write objectives that cover the intended answer and the intended learning. Read [Proposal Section 1](proposal-section1.md) when the student-facing wording sounds too much like a completed dissertation. A strong set normally states: the question/hypothesis; the main concepts or cases/data; the method; the intended analysis; the outcome; and one or two skills the student will practise. Keep `what I will find out`, `how I will investigate it`, and `what I will produce` distinct. Preserve necessary subject terms, but use clear plan-stage verbs and do not predetermine the final conclusion.

### Reasons for choosing the project

Connect personal interest, subject study or future plans, existing knowledge, and the importance of the question. The reason should explain why this student can sustain the project and why the question is worth investigating, not claim that the topic will solve a large social problem without evidence.

### Activities and timescales

Use an ordered sequence with task, purpose/output, duration or date range, and milestone. P301 normally includes question refinement, source search/evaluation, reading and synthesis, outline, drafting, revision, references, evaluation, and presentation. P302 must additionally make method design, data collection, data cleaning/analysis, calculations or modelling, and contingency work visible. Timescales should leave room for failed searches, rewriting, checking, and rehearsal.

### Resources, research areas, and contingencies

Name specific source classes and their intended use: peer-reviewed work, official statistics/reports, primary records, books, datasets, specialist databases, software, equipment, or people. State how credibility and relevance will be checked. For P302, specify data access, sample/instrument, permissions, storage, and what will happen if recruitment, equipment, or data quality fails. For P301, specify how conflicting definitions or outdated/biased sources will be handled.

## Activity Log entry pattern

For each real entry, write in first person and preserve the actual date:

`On [date], I [completed action] and produced/changed [evidence]. This mattered because [link to question/objective]. I noticed [problem, uncertainty, or decision]. I responded by [specific action], which changed [plan/method/source choice] where applicable. My next step is [action and reason].`

Good entries show development over time. They can record topic changes, search strategy changes, source rejection, feedback, method adjustments, failed attempts, data-quality issues, re-drafting, and formatting or presentation decisions when those decisions affect the outcome. A sequence of “I researched more and made progress” entries is not detailed management evidence.

For review, sample the log for: dated chronology; link to the question; tangible evidence; problems and solutions; plan-versus-reality comparison; justified changes; and a credible next step. Do not reward length alone.

## Evaluation or self-reflection pattern

Organise the evaluation around evidence, not a generic success narrative:

1. **Aim and outcome:** what the project set out to answer or make, what it achieved, and where the answer remains qualified.
2. **Management:** compare the original plan with actual progress; identify one delay or successful decision and its effect.
3. **Resources or method:** explain why important sources/data/methods were chosen, what was rejected, and how authority, currency, bias, validity, reliability, sample, or access affected interpretation.
4. **Outcome quality:** evaluate structure, reasoning, synthesis, calculations, figures, or product decisions with a concrete example.
5. **Limitations:** name the exact stage and consequence, not just “lack of time” or “small sample”.
6. **Improvement:** give an action, rationale, and expected benefit for the next iteration or study.
7. **Transfer:** state one process lesson that can be used in later study, supported by what actually changed during this project.

For P302, discuss data quality, sampling, controls/variables, measurement or model assumptions, calculations, and generalisability. For P301, discuss source coverage, synthesis, competing interpretations, and the limits of secondary research. Keep the reflection in the student's voice and do not manufacture emotion or personal growth.

## Paper review output

Return a compact report with:

- `判定与假设`: unit, version, evidence included, and missing evidence.
- `AO1-AO4`: provisional band/mark or `not reliably assessable`, with 1-3 evidence-linked reasons per AO.
- `Paper quality`: question alignment, source/resource quality, method/data quality, analysis/synthesis, structure and language, conclusion, limitations, citations, and integrity/AI record.
- `Highest-value revisions`: three changes stated as action + target section + why it raises the evidence level.
- `Generation option`: if requested, provide replacement paragraphs or form text only after separating verified facts from placeholders.

Do not use a fixed “A* formula”. A high mark requires the top-band quality across the relevant evidence, and grade boundaries are not supplied by these local files.
