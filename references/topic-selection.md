# Edexcel EPQ Topic Selection and Handoff

Use this reference when a learner has only a broad interest, several possible topics, or an unsettled project type. It is a pre-Proposal teaching layer for Edexcel P301-P304. It does not replace the Edexcel specification or award marks.

## Stage -1 readiness and intake

Before generating a final topic, establish only the facts that materially change the decision:

- project stage and target submission cycle;
- Edexcel project type if known: P301 Dissertation, P302 Investigation/Field Study, P303 Performance, or P304 Artefact;
- subject, future-study direction, skills, and genuine interests;
- existing sources, data, materials, equipment, venue, participants, or relevant experience;
- available time, place, access limits, and any privacy, consent, safeguarding, or safety issue.

If the learner gives only a broad noun such as “AI”, “medicine”, or “business”, do not immediately write a Proposal. Convert it into a small set of search terms and ask for the missing object, population/case, period, place, central variable, comparison, or creative problem.

Do not force a project type before the question or brief is researchable. Once the question is sufficiently clear, explain whether P301, P302, P303, or P304 is the better fit and ask the learner to confirm.

## Select a topic route

Choose one route based on the learner's starting point. These are flexible teaching heuristics, not Edexcel requirements.

### A. Interest anchoring

Use when the learner has a concrete interest, experience, article, book, object, or problem. Move from the interest to a debatable or realisable question by adding a defined context, comparison, mechanism, consequence, design constraint, or performance problem. Do not manufacture a trend or claim of importance merely to make the title sound academic.

### B. Three-source hybrid

Use when the learner knows a broad subject or future direction but has no settled question. Combine three different starting inputs: a genuine interest, a problem or tension, and a current or authoritative source. Generate several candidates, then let the learner reject or retain them using evidence access, scope, method, and personal ownership.

### C. Four-dimensional narrowing

Use when the candidate is too broad. Narrow through the dimensions that matter for the project: time, place, object/population, and central issue or variable. For P302 also test whether the variable can be measured and whether the sample, instrument, procedure, and analysis are actually accessible. For P303/P304, replace data variables with the performance or artefact brief and production constraints.

## Candidate generation and minimum validation

Generate two to six plausible candidates when the learner has not yet made a decision. For each candidate, separate:

- topic area: the broad subject;
- central research question, hypothesis, commission, or design brief;
- objectives: the answerable steps or production requirements;
- project type and method;
- expected outcome and scope boundary.

Before recommending entry to Proposal, run a minimum validation:

1. Create two or three keyword groups in the language(s) needed for the search.
2. Check whether credible sources, data, materials, equipment, venue, or participants can be accessed within the deadline.
3. Record at least one source or resource that supports the candidate and one unresolved question, limitation, disagreement, or design challenge.
4. Check that the question has a defined object/case, period/place/population or production constraint, and a method appropriate to the project type.
5. Screen privacy, consent, safeguarding, legal, ethical, and physical-safety issues. Escalate approval questions to the tutor or centre; do not claim that approval has been granted.

Search results are leads, not evidence. Do not invent a source, dataset, interview, experiment, participant, or finding to make a candidate appear feasible.

## Internal feasibility screen

When useful, score each candidate from 0 to 2 on each dimension:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interest and academic fit | no credible ownership or fit | plausible but weak | specific, sustained fit |
| Evidence/resource access | unavailable or unverified | possible with a gap | concrete and accessible |
| Scope fit | too broad, narrow, or vague | needs substantial narrowing | bounded for the available time |
| Argument or creative space | only description/predetermined result | some tension or choice | genuine analysis, evaluation, or design choice |
| Method feasibility | method unavailable or unsafe | method needs confirmation | method and evidence route are describable |
| Ethics, privacy, and safety | unresolved material risk | risk needs escalation or redesign | manageable with a clear safeguard |

The total is an internal comparison tool, not an Edexcel mark or threshold. Explain every deduction and give the next validation action. Never convert the score into a guaranteed grade.

## Decision record

Preserve the reasoning that led to the Proposal. Record, at minimum:

- candidate and revised candidate title;
- central question/hypothesis/brief and objectives;
- project type decision and provisional reason;
- scope and method;
- evidence/resource search terms and what was found;
- selected candidate and rejected candidates with reasons;
- feasibility concerns and ethics/safety actions;
- next action, owner, and date only when supplied or confirmed.

This record is not a substitute for the Activity Log. It is the source for a truthful first Proposal draft and later Activity Log entries about refinement, rejection, validation, and plan changes.

## Handoff to Proposal

Pass the confirmed parts into the Proposal workflow:

| Topic stage | Proposal destination |
| --- | --- |
| final candidate and scope | title/working title and project scope |
| central question/hypothesis/brief | project question or brief |
| objectives | Proposal objectives |
| interest, subject, and future-study connection | reasons for choosing the project |
| evidence/resource route and method | resources, research areas, and method |
| feasibility concerns and alternatives | contingencies, risks, ethics, and alternatives |
| decision record and next actions | initial Activity Log plan |

Do not silently resolve contradictions during handoff. Show the conflicting fields and ask the learner to confirm which decision changed. When facts are missing, use `[待确认]` or `[TO CONFIRM]` and list the evidence needed.

## Output modes

For a learner, return: recommended route, two to six candidates when needed, the distinction between topic/question/objectives, evidence to verify, an internal feasibility screen if useful, and the next validation task.

For a teacher, return: candidate comparison, method/project-type reasoning, evidence and access gaps, ethics or safety escalation, rejected-candidate rationale, and whether the learner is ready to enter Proposal.

For direct generation, do not produce a final Proposal until the question, scope, method, outcome, and key risks are confirmed. A provisional draft may be produced with placeholders, followed by `Evidence to verify` and `Facts still needed`.
